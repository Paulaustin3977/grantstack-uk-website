Chapter 7 — Disabled Facilities Grant (DFG)

Up to £30,000 of adaptations paid by your local council in England, £36,000 in Wales. Means-tested for adults, not means-tested for under-19s. The gateway is an Occupational Therapist assessment — here's how to start one.

Action summary

If someone in your home has a long-term illness, disability or is aged and struggling with daily living, ask your local council social services for an Occupational Therapist (OT) assessment. That single phone call is the gateway to up to £30,000 of council-funded home adaptations.

What it is

A mandatory grant paid by your local council in England and Wales towards the cost of essential home adaptations for disabled people. Means-tested for adults, not means-tested for children under 19. Funded from a £723m national budget for 2026/27.

Eligible works (typical)

  • Level-access showers and wet rooms.
  • Stairlifts and through-floor lifts.
  • Ramps and door widening.
  • Specialist bathing equipment.
  • Some kitchen adaptations.
  • Some heating changes where they are part of an OT-approved adaptation plan.

Who runs it

Your local council's housing office in England and Wales. The Department of Health & Social Care sets the framework; the funding is mandatory — every council must offer it.

Grant amounts (2026/27)

  • England: up to £30,000 per adaptation.
  • Wales: up to £36,000 per adaptation.
  • Multiple adaptations can each have their own DFG application.

Eligibility

  • A person (of any age) in the household has a disability or long-term illness that requires the adaptation.
  • The property is the disabled person's main residence (owner-occupier, private rented, or social housing tenant).
  • The proposed work is necessary and appropriate, and reasonable in relation to the property.
  • Means-tested for adults (with some means-test disregards for disability-related costs); not means-tested for under-19s.

How to apply

  1. Contact your local council social services — ask for an OT assessment. Phone, online form, or referral from a GP or social worker.
  2. The OT produces a recommendation listing the works and the assessed needs.
  3. You submit a DFG application to your local council housing office.
  4. The council decides. Statutory timescales vary by council; most are 6-12 weeks but can run longer for complex adaptations.
  5. If approved, the council pays the contractor directly on completion.

Stacking note

DFG is a separate scheme from the BUS and Warm Homes Local Grant. They can be combined where the measures are different (e.g. a stairlift + heat pump are different adaptations). Where the same measure (e.g. a wet-room floor rebuild that involves heating work) overlaps, DFG takes priority per the DESNZ "no double funding" rule.

Common pitfalls

  • Wait for the council decision before buying or installing equipment. Grants won't reimburse retrospective work.
  • Children under 19 are NOT means-tested — check eligibility regardless of household income.
  • Multiple applications for different adaptations are allowed.
  • Landlord consent is needed for tenants.
  • Some charities top up the DFG if the cost exceeds the cap — ask your OT.
