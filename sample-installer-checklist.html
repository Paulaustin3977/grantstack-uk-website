Chapter 10 — Quote and installer workflow

The single biggest determinant of value is the installer. Get at least three written quotes before you sign anything. Here are the 10 questions to ask each installer — and the exact words to use to make sure the BUS grant appears as a line-by-line deduction on every quote.

Action summary

Get three written quotes from MCS-certified installers (verify at mcscertified.org.uk). Ask each one the 10 questions below in the same order, so you can compare answers side-by-side. Never accept a "discount" instead of a line-by-line BUS deduction.

The 10 questions to ask every installer

  1. Are you MCS-certified for this technology? Show me the certificate.
  2. Are you currently onboarded into the BUS portal? Some installers have an MCS certificate but have not completed BUS onboarding.
  3. Will the BUS grant be deducted on the invoice, line by line? A good installer breaks it down as gross cost — £7,500 BUS = your invoice.
  4. What is the heat pump manufacturer's warranty, and what is the installer's workmanship warranty?
  5. How long from quotation to installation? Q4 of 2026 / Q1 of 2027 will be the BUS peak — wait times are extending.
  6. Will you issue an MCS commissioning certificate on completion?
  7. Have you completed a Warm Homes Local Grant project this year? Experienced with stacking schemes, not just BUS.
  8. What happens if Ofgem rejects the application after installation? A good installer has a clear answer. A bad installer shrugs.
  9. Can I see your last three BUS redemptions (with customer consent)? Real installers have evidence.
  10. Who owns the system after year five, year ten, year twenty? This catches third-party-owned arrangements which are excluded from BUS.

Compare like-for-like

Heat pump installation costs vary by 100% between installers for genuinely equivalent work. £8,000 vs £15,000 for the same install is not unusual. Same heat pump model, same radiators, same commissioning — but very different prices.

When you have three quotes, line them up in a single spreadsheet:

  • Gross cost
  • BUS grant deducted
  • Net to you
  • Heat pump brand + model + SCOP (Seasonal Coefficient of Performance)
  • Radiator sizing scope
  • Heat pump cylinder included?
  • Controls included (thermostat, weather compensation)
  • Installation timeline (weeks)
  • Warranty years (manufacturer + workmanship)
  • Optional add-ons (underfloor heating loop, smart thermostat, mains upgrade)

Compare efficiency figures too

A cheaper install with a 2.5 SCOP is not the same as a 4.0 SCOP. SCOP is the Seasonal Coefficient of Performance — how much heat the pump delivers per unit of electricity across a typical UK year. A higher SCOP means lower running costs forever.

Rough rule of thumb: a 1.0 SCOP difference on an air source heat pump saves roughly £250-£400/year in running costs for a typical UK 3-bed semi. Over the 15-20 year life of the system, the difference between a 2.5 SCOP and a 4.0 SCOP install is £4,000-£8,000.

Cheap is not always cheap.

The two-pipeline test

Any installer who objects to your 10 questions, refuses to put BUS on the invoice line by line, or pushes back on you getting a second or third quote is one you walk away from. The good installers welcome the questions — they are the installers who get most of their work through referrals.

After you've picked one

  1. Sign the installer's contract (your own paperwork).
  2. Wait for the Ofgem consent email. Open the secure link. Authorise.
  3. Install happens (3 months for air/biomass, 6 months for ground source).
  4. Installer submits the BUS redemption to Ofgem.
  5. Ofgem pays the installer. Your invoice was already reduced.
  6. You receive the MCS commissioning certificate and the manufacturer's warranty paperwork.
