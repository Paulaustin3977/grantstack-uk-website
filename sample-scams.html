Chapter 9 — Scam warnings, and how to verify a real grant

UK home-energy grants attract the highest volume of consumer-facing scams per pound of any government scheme, partly because the sums are large. Three scam patterns are common. Here is how to spot each, and the official numbers to call when in doubt.

Action summary

If anything about a "free grant" offer rings a bell from this chapter — phone number, deposit request, or a "we'll handle the paperwork, just give us a deposit" — stop and call Ofgem directly at 0330 053 2006 or the Warm Homes Local Grant helpline at 0800 098 7950. Both are free, both are real, and both will tell you whether the offer you received is legitimate.

The three scam patterns to know

1. The "free phone number" scam

You receive a cold call offering to "check your eligibility for a £7,500 grant". You are charged for the call. The grant does not exist or you do not qualify.

There is no government phone line for this. Hang up. Real BUS / Warm Homes Local Grant schemes are applied to either via your installer or directly through GOV.UK portals — never through a cold call.

2. The "deposit to release the grant" scam

You are told you need to pay a deposit to release a grant. Sometimes via bank transfer. Sometimes via gift cards (a common giveaway for any scam).

No legitimate scheme requires a deposit to release a grant. The BUS is paid directly to the installer and deducted from your invoice. Warm Homes Local Grant is paid directly to the contractor by your local council. You never handle the grant money.

3. The "MCS — we will do the paperwork" scam

An installer quotes you a high price but offers to "handle the BUS paperwork and we pass the grant through to you". They pocket the grant. The grant is then 100% in their hands with no audit trail you can verify.

Ask for the grant deduction to appear on the invoice itself, line by line. A real installer breaks it down: gross cost — £7,500 BUS = your invoice. A scam installer quotes a high price with a hidden "discount" they keep.

How to verify a real grant

  1. The scheme should appear on gov.uk. Use gov.uk search, not a third party site. The BUS, Warm Homes Local Grant and Crisis and Resilience Fund all appear there.
  2. There should be no application fee. Anyone charging a fee to "process" a GOV.UK grant is not legitimate.
  3. The grant should be deducted from your invoice, not paid as a separate cash transfer to you.
  4. Verify an installer at mcscertified.org.uk — if they are not on the register, they cannot claim BUS.
  5. Verify a lender on the DESNZ Warm Homes Loan Scheme participating-lender list (gov.uk).

What to do when in doubt

Email Ofgem at BUS.enquiry@ofgem.gov.uk or call 0330 053 2006.

Call the Warm Homes Local Grant helpline on 0800 098 7950 (Mon–Fri 8am–6pm, Sat 9am–12pm).

For crisis grants, call your local council's main switchboard and ask for the "Crisis and Resilience Fund" or "local welfare assistance".

Reporting a scam

  • Action Fraud (UK national fraud reporting centre): actionfraud.police.uk or 0300 123 2040.
  • Citizens Advice Consumer Service: citizensadvice.org.uk/consumer for general consumer scams.
  • Your local council trading standards team for doorstep or local-business scams.

The honest caveat

The scammers are quick, professional, and persuasive. They update their pitch every time a new grant launches. The only realistic defence is to never share your personal details, never pay anything, and never trust a single source. Always cross-reference with the official GOV.UK page for the scheme in question.

Verified sources

  • gov.uk — Warm Homes: Local Grant apply page
  • Action Fraud — actionfraud.police.uk
  • Citizens Advice Consumer Service — citizensadvice.org.uk/consumer
  • MCS contractor register — mcscertified.org.uk
